@@ intro
A byte is two halves of 4 bits ("nibbles"). swap_bits exchanges them:

    0100 | 0001   =>   0001 | 0100          65 => 20

Take the high half, take the low half, put them back in the other order.

@@ gap 1
answer: octet >> 4
accept: octet / 16
wrong: octet << 4 => Shifting left pushes the high half OUT of the byte. Shift right to bring it down.
wrong: octet & 0xF0 => That keeps the high half in place (e.g. 01000000). Shift it down: octet >> 4.
hint: Move the 4 high bits down into the low positions.
`octet >> 4` drops the 4 low bits and moves the high half down: 01000001 >> 4 = 00000100.

@@ gap 2
answer: octet & 0x0F
accept: octet & 15
accept: octet & 0xF
accept: octet % 16
accept: octet & 0x0f
wrong: octet & 0xF0 => 0xF0 = 11110000 keeps the HIGH half. The low half is 0x0F = 00001111.
wrong: octet >> 4 => That's the high half again.
hint: A mask that keeps only the 4 low bits.
`& 0x0F` (binary 00001111) keeps the 4 low bits and clears the others.
Hexadecimal is handy for masks: each hex digit is exactly 4 bits.

@@ gap 3
answer: (low << 4) | high
accept: low << 4 | high
accept: high | (low << 4)
accept: (low << 4) + high
accept: low * 16 + high
wrong: (high << 4) | low => That rebuilds the original byte. The halves must change places.
wrong: low | high => Both halves end up in the low bits: shift the low half up first.
hint: The old low half goes up, the old high half stays down.
`low << 4` moves the low half into the high position, and `| high` fills the low
position with the old high half:

    low  = 0001  =>  low << 4 = 00010000
    high = 0100  =>  | high   = 00010100  = 20

The whole function also fits in one line: `return ((octet >> 4) | (octet << 4));`.

@@ outro
Bit toolbox:
- `octet >> 4` = high nibble, `octet & 0x0F` = low nibble
- `x << 4` moves a nibble into the high position
- `|` combines bits that don't overlap
