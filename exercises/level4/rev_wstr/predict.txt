@@ question
match: exact
args: ["Wingardium Leviosa"]
answer: Leviosa Wingardium$
    $> ./rev_wstr "Wingardium Leviosa" | cat -e
---
The WORDS come out in reverse order, separated by one space, followed by "\n".

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["abcdefghijklm"]
answer: abcdefghijklm$
wrong: mlkjihgfedcba$ => rev_wstr reverses the order of the WORDS, not the letters. One word stays exactly the same.
    $> ./rev_wstr "abcdefghijklm" | cat -e
---
Only the order of the words changes, never the letters inside a word. A single
word is printed unchanged.

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["You hate people! But I love gatherings."]
answer: gatherings. love I But people! hate You$
    $> ./rev_wstr "You hate people! But I love gatherings." | cat -e
---
A word is everything between spaces - punctuation included. "people!" and
"gatherings." are words, the `!` and `.` stay attached to them.

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["Hello", "World"]
answer: $
wrong: World Hello$ => Those are two separate PARAMETERS. The program takes exactly one; with 2 it only prints a newline.
    $> ./rev_wstr "Hello" "World" | cat -e
---
Two parameters (argc == 3) => "the program will display '\n'". The quotes matter:
"Hello World" would be one parameter with two words.

@@ question
match: exact
args: [""]
answer: $
    $> ./rev_wstr "" | cat -e
---
One parameter, but it contains no words: nothing to print except the newline.

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["1 22 333"]
answer: 333 22 1$
    $> ./rev_wstr "1 22 333" | cat -e
---
Words are reversed as a whole: "333" stays "333". One space between words, none
at the end - `cat -e` would show a trailing space as "1 $".
