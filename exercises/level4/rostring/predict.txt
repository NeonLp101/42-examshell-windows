@@ question
match: exact
args: ["abc   "]
answer: abc$
wrong: abc   $ => Trailing blanks are not part of any word - they are not printed.
wrong: abc $ => Only one word: nothing comes before it, so no separator is printed.
    $> ./rostring "abc   " | cat -e
---
A single word: there are no "other words" to print first, so the output is just
the word. The trailing spaces separate nothing, so they disappear - no space before the `$`.

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["   hello     big   world  "]
answer: big world hello$
wrong: big     world hello$ => Several blanks between words become exactly ONE space in the output.
wrong:  big world hello$ => Leading blanks are ignored - the output starts directly with the first printed word.
    $> ./rostring "   hello     big   world  " | cat -e
---
"Words will be separated by only one space in the output": leading and trailing
blanks vanish, and runs of blanks collapse into one space.

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["     AkjhZ zLKIJz , 23y"]
answer: zLKIJz , 23y AkjhZ$
    $> ./rostring "     AkjhZ zLKIJz , 23y" | cat -e
---
The lonely "," is a word too: a word is anything between blanks. So the words are
AkjhZ | zLKIJz | , | 23y, and AkjhZ moves to the end.

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["first", "2", "11000000"]
answer: first$
wrong: $ => Unlike fprime and rev_wstr, rostring only prints "\n" when there is LESS than one argument. Extra arguments are ignored.
wrong: 2 11000000 first$ => Only argv[1] is rotated. The other arguments are ignored.
    $> ./rostring "first" "2" "11000000" | cat -e
---
Read the subject word by word: "If there's less than one argument, the program
displays \n." With 3 arguments the program just uses the first one - a single
word, printed as is.

@@ question
match: exact
args: ["one\ttwo\tthree"]
answer: two three one$
    $> ./rostring $'one\ttwo\tthree' | cat -e
---
In bash, `$'...'` turns `\t` into a real tab. Tabs separate words just like spaces,
but the output always uses single spaces.
